# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Sports / athletics club members: players, team captains, and admins. Members are engaged, competitive, and club-proud — they open the app before practice, the morning of an event, or whenever a notification lands. Admins keep the machine running: managing rosters, posting announcements, and reviewing grievances. The context is casual-to-urgent: checking scores and RSVPs on mobile, managing rosters on desktop.

## Product Purpose

KUPA is the digital home base for an athletics club. It replaces the group chat, the spreadsheet roster, and the email announcement — putting events, teams, notices, merchandise, and internal feedback in one place. Success looks like a member opening KUPA and knowing exactly what's happening this week and where they stand on their team.

## Brand Personality

Bold, energetic, competitive. The energy of a locker room — confident, direct, team-focused. Not a tool you tolerate; a platform you're proud to be on.

## References

ESPN / Sky Sports: broadcast sports energy — strong hierarchy, team colors used with purpose, information that reads at a glance. The "this matters" visual weight of a live scoreboard.

## Anti-references

- Generic corporate / HR tools (intranet gray, Microsoft Teams default, boring tabular layouts)
- Kids / cartoon-style apps (bubbly type, pastel palettes, friendly mascots)
- Sterile SaaS dashboards (startup-template white, metric-hero grids, card-grid-on-white with no personality)

## Design Principles

1. **Scoreboard hierarchy** — every screen has one dominant piece of information. The eye lands there first, always. Competing focal points are wrong.
2. **Earned red** — the primary red is club identity, not decoration. Use it with discipline so every red moment means something: a primary action, a live status, a critical alert.
3. **Team first** — belonging to a team is the core emotional hook. Design surfaces that make the team feel real and the member's role within it legible.
4. **No spectators** — every screen is an invitation to act: RSVP, join, post, respond. Dead ends and empty states must redirect into participation.
5. **Mobile-native pacing** — members check in on phones. Every layout earns its mobile form first; desktop is a spacious extension, not the primary canvas.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG 2.1 AA: minimum 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large/bold text and UI components. Keyboard navigability for all interactive elements. Motion should respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Formalize requirements when the user base is better defined.
